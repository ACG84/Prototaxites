# Do foraminifera actually require a glycoprotein wall?

Prompted by the objection that the chitin/glycoprotein argument against H3 was overstated. **It was —
on chitin.** This document says how much, and shows why the glycoprotein half does not fall with it.

Primary source: **Tyszka J, Godos K, Goleń J, Radmacher W (2021), "Foraminiferal organic linings:
functional and phylogenetic challenges," *Earth-Science Reviews* 220:103726** (CC-BY), read in full.

---

## 1. The chitin correction — conceded

Earlier notes in this repo said foraminiferal organic linings contain chitin derivatives across taxa
and would therefore classify with Loron et al.'s chitinous group. That was too strong, and Tyszka et
al. say so directly:

> Linings "are usually interpreted as chitinous, chitinoid, pseudochitinous or tectinous... **although
> their composition was never elucidated in detail**."

Supporting the same point:

- **Allen et al. (2000)** compared glycoprotein reference standards against foraminiferal organic
  matrix and found the material "similar to **collagen rather than to chitin**, with slight variations
  from one species to another."
- **Hedley (1963)** had already criticised "tectin" and "pseudochitin" as chemically undefined.
- Chitin *is* confirmed in specific taxa — Ní Fhlaithearta et al. (2013) found chitin derivatives, plus
  traces of guaiacols and syringols, in *Calcarina* and *Cycloclypeus* — but as taxon-level findings,
  not a group-wide property.

**Chitin in Foraminifera is variable and was never properly established as general. The earlier claim
was wrong.**

---

## 2. Not all foraminifera produce organic linings — also true, and irrelevant

Tyszka et al.'s opening question is literally "Do all foraminiferal classes produce organic linings?"
The answer is no:

- **Spirillina** produces none, so cannot enter the palynological record as a lining.
- Linings are vanishingly rare for **Tubothalamea** (miliolids and relatives) — only a single extracted
  *Quinqueloculina* lining (Taugourdeau-Lantz & Poignant 1964).
- Dissolution experiments (Cohen & Guber 1968; Traverse & Ginsburg 1966) show far fewer species yield
  linings than exist in the source fauna.
- Table 2 of the review marks much of the Tubothalamea composition column **"never studied"** — so part
  of this "absence" is ignorance, not biology.

But this does not help H3, for the reason given before and now confirmed by the review's own framing:
**these taxa dispense with an organic lining because a mineral test is doing the structural work.**
Losing the lining is a consequence of calcification, not an alternative organic chemistry.
*Prototaxites* has no mineral phase at all.

---

## 3. The gap I claimed was open is closed — by Allen et al. (2000)

An earlier draft of this document argued that the chemical case against H3 rested on extrapolating
from calcareous rotaliids to organic-walled monothalamids, and that nobody had tested the relevant
grade. **That was wrong, and the review says so.**

> "Their spectral techniques, applied to **agglutinated foraminifera**, i.e. ***Astrammina rara***
> Rhumbler, *Jadammina macrescens* (Brady), *Trochammina inflata* Montagu, and calcareous foraminifera
> represented by *Ammonia beccarii* (Linné), **confirmed that foraminiferal organic linings and organic
> cements of agglutinated foraminifera are composed mainly of glycoproteinaceous materials**.
> *A. rara* revealed **infrared absorption spectra** that was most similar to collagen."

***Astrammina rara* is a monothalamid astrorhizid** — the organic-cemented, agglutinated,
allogromiid-grade foraminiferan that H3 requires. It has been analysed by **infrared spectroscopy**,
the same technique class Loron et al. used. The answer came back **glycoproteinaceous**.

Three further passages close the door:

1. **The extrapolation is licensed explicitly.** "The organic matrix in calcareous shells **likely
   consists of the same organic material** as that present in the taxa with **tectinous and
   agglutinated walls**" (Angell 1967a; Banner et al. 1973; Mikhalevich 2014).
2. **Mucopolysaccharides are the structural default.** The IOL is "mainly construct[ed]" by
   mucopolysaccharides with charged functional groups (Moss 1964; Angell 1967a; Spindler 1978).
3. **It is the ancestral condition.** Haynes (1981) derived the inner organic lining of advanced
   foraminifera from "organic tests of more primitive **allogromiids (monothalamids)**," and
   Ferràndez-Cañadell (2002), following Le Calvez (1947), tied the **mucopolysaccharide** lining to
   "the **ancestral organic test that evolved before appearance of mineral skeletons**."

So the organic-walled monothalamid grade is not an unexplored refuge for a non-glycoprotein wall. It
is where the review locates the **ancestral glycoprotein/mucopolysaccharide condition** of the entire
group.

## 4. Why the chitin correction does not rescue the hypothesis

Loron et al.'s discriminator was never a chitin band. It is:

> "fossilization products resulting from the alteration of **sugar-protein compounds**, especially the
> **carbonyl and nitrogen moieties**"

Chitin is one route to that signal; **protein is another**. Replacing "chitin" with "collagen-like" —
the exact substitution the disputing literature proposes — leaves the discriminating element,
**nitrogen**, untouched, and arguably strengthens it. Collagen is nitrogen-dense with strong amide I
and II absorption.

The review also quantifies how much protein is there: average amino acid content ~2523 nmol/g
(planktic) and ~2808 nmol/g (benthic). And the user-supplied **Prada et al. (2024)**, *PNAS*
121(50):e2417845121, identified **373 test-bound proteins** in *Amphistegina lobifera*, strongly
enriched in aspartic and glutamic acid. (Fairly: that is intracrystalline matrix in a calcareous
rotaliid — a small mass fraction, not the monothalamid grade at issue. Not decisive, but not helpful
to H3 either.)

**And the word that never appears in this review: "aromatic." Nor "phenolic."** A comprehensive
2021 review of foraminiferal organic composition — glycosaminoglycans, over-sulfated GAGs,
glycoproteins, collagen-like proteins, heterogeneous proteins, polysaccharides, unidentified
substances — contains no report of an aromatic or polyphenolic structural biopolymer anywhere in
Foraminifera.

That is what *P. taiti* is made of.

## 5. Direct answer to the question

**Is there a foraminiferan known to lack glycoprotein-type structures?**

Sorting the cases:

| Case | Why it lacks a glycoprotein lining | Use to H3 |
|---|---|---|
| *Spirillina*, miliolids/tubothalameans | Mineral (calcite) test does the structural work | None — wrong reason |
| Silicoloculinida (*Miliammellus legis*) | Opaline silica test, self-secreted | None — mineral, not organic |
| Agglutinated, ferruginous/calcareous cement | Cement mineralised by Ca and Fe | None — mineral |
| Organic-walled monothalamids (*Astrammina rara*) | **Does not lack it** — IR-confirmed glycoproteinaceous | Closes the refuge |
| Tubothalamean composition generally | "Never studied" | Unknown, not evidence |

Every foraminiferan that dispenses with a glycoprotein organic wall does so **by biomineralising**.
Not one replaces it with a different structural *organic* polymer.

**There is no known foraminiferan that lacks glycoprotein-type structures and builds a purely organic
body.** That specific combination is what *Prototaxites* requires — 100% thick-walled organic tubes,
1–2 µm walls, no mineral phase, no agglutinated grains, aliphatic/aromatic/phenolic chemistry with no
polysaccharide band ensemble and no sugar–protein recondensation products. The taxa satisfying the
first half satisfy it by failing the second.

## 6. What genuinely remains open

Two things, and it is worth being precise that neither is what was asked.

1. **The xenophyophore granellare sheath has never been characterised.** The word "xenophyophore"
   does not appear in Tyszka et al. at all. That is a real hole — but it bears on H1/H2, already
   falsified in [`rhizaria-hypothesis.md`](rhizaria-hypothesis.md) on xenophyae, stercomare, habitat
   and septation, so filling it changes nothing.
2. **Tubothalamean organic composition is largely "never studied."** But tubothalameans are
   calcareous tubular forms — the least *Prototaxites*-like grade in the phylum.

Meanwhile the review supplies something useful in the other direction: **Devonian foraminiferal
organic linings are dominated by agglutinated monothalamids**, per Bell & Winchester-Seeto (1999).
The control material in [`control-experiment.md`](control-experiment.md) is therefore the *right
grade* as well as roughly the right age — the thermal-maturity screen remains the binding constraint.

---

## 7. Net effect on the verdict

H3 stays at **substantially weakened**, with the reasoning repaired rather than the conclusion
changed:

- The **chitin** argument is withdrawn. It was overstated and the review contradicts it.
- The **glycoprotein/nitrogen** argument survives intact, and is now better supported: the
  monothalamid grade has been IR-tested and is glycoproteinaceous; mucopolysaccharide is the
  reconstructed ancestral foraminiferal wall material; no aromatic or phenolic structural biopolymer
  is reported anywhere in the phylum.
- The **wall-lability** argument I conceded earlier stands as a correction, but the direction of that
  lability is uniformly toward biomineralisation, never toward structural organic aromatics.
- The **Rhynie *Palaeoleptochlamys* control** is untouched by any of this, since it does not route
  through foraminiferal chemistry at all.

The objection improved the argument's accuracy without moving its conclusion. That is worth
something, but it is not a rescue.
