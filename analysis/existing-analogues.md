# Are there existing protist walls with a *P. taiti*-like chemistry?

Searching for a positive analogue rather than arguing from absence. The answer splits: protist wall
chemistry is far more diverse than "tectin = glycoprotein" implies, and one group has converged on
plant-type resistant chemistry — but the single feature *P. taiti* is most extreme in is the one
feature every characterised protist wall retains.

---

## 1. Within Rhizaria: nothing

Every foraminiferal or gromiid wall anyone has measured comes back protein plus mucopolysaccharide.

| Taxon | Result |
|---|---|
| *Allogromia laticollaris* | >60% protein, ~13% sugars |
| *Astrammina rara* (monothalamid) | Glycoproteinaceous, collagen-like (Allen et al. 2000, by IR) |
| *Jadammina*, *Trochammina* | Glycoproteinaceous |
| *Ammonia beccarii* lining | Glycoproteinaceous |
| *Gromia* | Proteinaceous + acid mucopolysaccharide + lipid |

No documented rhizarian analogue. That much is settled.

---

## 2. Outside Rhizaria: dinosporin is the real find

**Dinoflagellate cyst walls** are the best-studied protist resistant biopolymer, and the picture is
much richer than the foraminiferal one.

**The key dataset:** *Unraveling the chemical composition of modern, resistant, organic-walled
dinoflagellate cysts via FTIR spectroscopy* — **216 ATR-FTIR spectra, 10 families, 25 genera, 51
species, 17 localities.** Same technique as Loron et al.

Findings that matter here:

- Dinosporin is **"more variable than previously thought,"** requiring **four distinct spectrochemical
  groups**. It is best understood as "a suite of related but chemically distinguishable biopolymers,"
  and species *within the same genus* differ.
- **Some groups show "striking similarities to other resistant biomacromolecules such as sporopollenin
  and algaenan."**
- **Eumelanin** is likely present in coloured cysts, acting as a UV screen.

### Why this helps the hypothesis

It is a proof of concept, and a good one. **Protists demonstrably evolve bespoke, highly resistant wall
biopolymers, taxon-specific down to congeners, and some of them converge on plant-type resistant
chemistry.** Dinosporin is "a completely different biomacromolecule" from algaenan — dinoflagellates
invented their own.

So the objection "no protist builds a sporopollenin-like wall" is simply false, and the broader
objection "a rhizarian could not evolve a novel resistant wall polymer" loses most of its force. Until
dinosporin was characterised, nobody knew dinoflagellates did this either.

The **eumelanin** result reinforces the aromatic-protein direction in
[`aromatic-protein-hypothesis.md`](aromatic-protein-hypothesis.md): melanin is tyrosine-derived, and
protists do incorporate tyrosine-derived aromatic polymer into resistant walls.

### Why it does not close the case

> **"Nitrogen is present in all observed dinosporin types."**

Every one of the four spectrochemical groups — including the sporopollenin-like ones — retains
nitrogen. And the dinosporin backbone is described as "a heavily cross-linked, **N-containing**,
cellulose-like macromolecule."

That is the wrong direction on **both** of *P. taiti*'s defining absences: it is N-bearing, and it is
carbohydrate-based, while *P. taiti* shows neither the amide signature nor the polysaccharide band
ensemble at 1200–800 cm⁻¹.

Melanin does not rescue this either — melanin is itself nitrogen-containing (indole nitrogen).

## 2b. Checking the dinocyst dataset directly

Retrieved and read: **Meyvisch P, Mertens K, Gurdebeke PR, Sandt C, Pospelova V, Vrielinck H,
Borondics F, Louwye S (2023). "Does dinocyst wall composition really reflect trophic affinity? New
evidence from ATR micro-FTIR spectroscopy measurements." *Journal of Phycology* 59(5):1064–1084,
doi:10.1111/jpy.13382.** Open copy at https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00851/96259/106781.pdf

211 cysts, 10 families, 24 genera, 50 species, 17 localities, ATR micro-FTIR over 4000–600 cm⁻¹.

### The four groups

| Group | Taxa | Chemistry |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — basic dinosporin** | Transparent gonyaulacaleans (*Impagidinium*, *Lingulodinium*, *Operculodinium*, *Spiniferites*…) | Cellulose-like backbone (CLB) + weak carbonyl |
| **2 — coloured** | Peridinialeans + gymnodinialeans | CLB + **secondary amines** from pigment, matched to a *Sepia* **eumelanin** standard |
| **3 — aromatic** | ***Trinovantedinium applanatum* only** | CLB + **aromatic rings**; "similarities with **sporopollenin**" |
| **4 — aliphatic** | *Fusiperidinium wisconsinense*, *Peridinium limbatum* (freshwater) | CLB + prominent **aliphatic** bands; similar to **algaenan** |

### The decisive finding

> "some specific molecular components appear **ubiquitous in all specimens**: there is proof for
> hydrogen (hydroxyl) bonding, **amide groups**... and **β-1,4-linked polysaccharides**... suggesting
> that the backbone of dinosporin is a heavily cross-linked, **N-containing, cellulose-like
> macromolecule**."

> "N is present in the cyst walls of both auto- and heterotrophic dinoflagellates... apparent from the
> presence of **amide absorption bands in all spectra from the ATR dataset**."

**Groups 2–4 are basic dinosporin *plus* an added component. They are additions, not replacements.**
Even the aromatic group retains the amide-bearing, polysaccharide backbone underneath.

That settles the question, and the architecture is the point:

| | Backbone | Decoration |
|---|---|---|
| **Aromatic dinocyst** (*T. applanatum*) | N-bearing, β-1,4-polysaccharide | aromatic rings |
| ***P. taiti*** | **absent — no amide ensemble, no 1200–800 cm⁻¹ polysaccharide bands** | aromatic + aliphatic |

The one aromatic protist wall known to science **adds** aromatics on top of exactly the two components
*P. taiti* lacks. *P. taiti* has the decoration without the backbone. Group 4 is the wrong direction
too — *P. taiti* is aliphatic-*poor*. And Group 2's route to aromatics is eumelanin, which carries its
own nitrogen.

### Data access

The spectra are in **Supporting Information Table S2** (metadata Table S3), not a public repository.
Obtainable from the journal, but the band definitions differ from Loron et al.'s table S1, so
projecting into their six-feature space would require re-deriving intensities from the raw spectra
rather than reusing published values.

## 3. The closest chemical match is a land-plant polymer

Loron et al. describe the *P. taiti* macerate as containing "short/branched aliphatic moieties,
carbonyl/carboxyl, aromatic, and ether compounds."

**Sporopollenin** is composed of "phenolic, alkane, alkene, ketone, lactone, and carboxylic acid
groups" — the closest published match to that description among known resistant biopolymers.

But sporopollenin's **polyketide biosynthetic pathway appears specific to land plants
(Embryophyta)**. And **algaenan**, the sporopollenin-like biopolymer of some chlorophytes, is
aliphatic-dominated — whereas *P. taiti* sits at the aliphatic-poor end (CH₂ 0.2493, against 0.7095 for
cyanobacteria and 0.4047 for plants).

So the nearest analogue by composition is embryophyte chemistry, which is consistent with *P. taiti*
plotting near plants in the CCA and offers no protist route.

---

## 4. A parallel worth noting

**Chitinozoans** — Palaeozoic, flask-shaped, organic-walled, produced by "an as yet unknown organism,"
with a wall historically called **"pseudochitin"**: a chemically undefined placeholder, exactly like
tectin. An organic-walled Palaeozoic problematicum whose chemistry was never resolved and whose
affinity remains open. It is a reminder that this class of problem has a poor track record of being
solved by wall chemistry alone.

---

## 5. The most valuable thing this turned up

**The 216-spectrum dinocyst ATR-FTIR dataset is a directly comparable protist-wall comparison group.**

The taphonomically matched protist control in Loron et al. is **n = 3** (*Palaeoleptochlamys*), and I
showed in [`data-availability.md`](data-availability.md) §1.1 that only Amide I separates cleanly at
that sample size. Reducing the dinocyst spectra to the same six band intensities would give a protist
resistant-wall comparison set two orders of magnitude larger.

Caveat, and it is the same one as always: **these are modern cysts, not 407 Ma silicified fossils.**
The comparison is only legitimate after artificial maturation, or with an explicit maturity correction.
But it is the largest existing body of ATR-FTIR data on protist resistant walls, and nobody has put it
next to *Prototaxites*.

---

## 5b. How much the dinocyst result is actually worth

Not as much as its size suggests, and the limits are worth stating explicitly.

**Dinoflagellates are not foraminifera.** Alveolata and Rhizaria are separated by on the order of a
billion years. Wall chemistry is not conserved across that distance in any way that licenses inference
from one to the other. The dinocyst survey answers the question "does *any* characterised protist wall
look like *P. taiti*?" — and the answer is no — but it is not evidence about what a foraminiferan could
do.

**And the foraminiferal sampling underneath the chemical objection is very thin.** Counting the taxa
with any actual organic-wall chemistry:

*Allogromia laticollaris*, *Astrammina rara*, *Jadammina macrescens*, *Trochammina inflata*,
*Ammonia beccarii*, *Rosalina floridana*, *Calcarina*, *Cycloclypeus*, *Amphistegina lobifera*,
*Chilostomella* — plus *Gromia* as an outgroup.

That is **roughly ten taxa out of ~9,000–10,000 living species: about 0.1%.**

Worse, the sample is biased away from everything relevant. There is **no** chemical characterisation of:

- **deep-sea monothalamids** — the clades where molecular work finds the highest cryptic diversity
- **xenophyophore granellare** — zero, as established earlier
- **freshwater and soil monothalamids** — *Edaphoallogromia*, *Reticulomyxa*, *Haplomyxa*
- **any giant foraminiferan** — the size class the hypothesis actually concerns

So the defensible sentence is **"every foraminiferal organic wall anyone has measured is
glycoproteinaceous,"** not "foraminiferal organic walls are glycoproteinaceous." Those differ
substantially in strength, and this repository has been using the stronger one.

### What survives the correction

One thing, and it needs the right axis. **Foraminiferal chemical diversity is enormous in
*mineralisation* and conservative in *organic matrix*.**

| Axis | Diversity |
|---|---|
| Hard-part chemistry | Organic; agglutinated with organic, calcareous, siliceous or ferruginous cement; porcelaneous high-Mg calcite; hyaline perforate calcite; **opaline silica**; composite calcite + opal |
| Organic matrix chemistry | Protein + polysaccharide, in every measured case |

The diversity argument is true and applies to the first row. The chemical objection to H3′ concerns the
second. And while ten taxa is 0.1% of the clade, they are not a random ten — they span the
organic-walled monothalamid, agglutinated and calcareous grades, and were measured by IR, pyrolysis
GC-MS and proteomics independently.

That is better than 0.1% sounds, and still far from settled — because the unmeasured taxa are precisely
the deep-sea, giant, and non-marine ones the hypothesis depends on.

**Net calibration:** the chemical objection to a foraminiferal affinity should be held less firmly than
this repository has been holding it. It is not weakened by dinosporin, which was never about forams. It
is weakened by the fact that nobody has looked at the relevant taxa.

---

## 6. Verdict

**No existing analogue matches, and the dinocyst check makes that specific rather than general.**
Nothing in Rhizaria comes close. Dinosporin — the best protist candidate, now checked directly across
211 cysts and 50 species — carries amide bands and β-1,4-polysaccharide bands in **every spectrum**,
including the aromatic and algaenan-like groups. The single known aromatic protist wall builds
aromatics *onto* that backbone; *P. taiti* has aromatics *instead of* it. The closest compositional
match to *P. taiti* remains sporopollenin, a land-plant polymer.

**But the search materially improves the hypothesis's position on one point.** "A protist could not
evolve a novel, plant-like resistant wall polymer" is refuted — dinoflagellates did exactly that, with
four distinct chemistries, some sporopollenin-like, and one incorporating a tyrosine-derived aromatic
pigment.

What survives as the constraint is narrower and sharper than before: **not "protists don't make
resistant aromatic walls," but "every protist resistant wall so far characterised retains nitrogen."**
That is the axis the whole disagreement collapsed onto in
[`tectin-variants.md`](tectin-variants.md), and it is where the argument now lives.
