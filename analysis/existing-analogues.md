# Are there existing protist walls with a *P. taiti*-like chemistry?

Searching for a positive analogue rather than arguing from absence. The answer splits: protist wall
chemistry is far more diverse than "tectin = glycoprotein" implies, and one group has converged on
plant-type resistant chemistry — but the single feature *P. taiti* is most extreme in is the one
feature every characterised protist wall retains.

---

## 1. Within Rhizaria: nothing

Every foraminiferal or gromiid wall anyone has measured comes back protein plus mucopolysaccharide.

| Taxon | Result |
|---|---|
| *Allogromia laticollaris* | >60% protein, ~13% sugars |
| *Astrammina rara* (monothalamid) | Glycoproteinaceous, collagen-like (Allen et al. 2000, by IR) |
| *Jadammina*, *Trochammina* | Glycoproteinaceous |
| *Ammonia beccarii* lining | Glycoproteinaceous |
| *Gromia* | Proteinaceous + acid mucopolysaccharide + lipid |

No documented rhizarian analogue. That much is settled.

---

## 2. Outside Rhizaria: dinosporin is the real find

**Dinoflagellate cyst walls** are the best-studied protist resistant biopolymer, and the picture is
much richer than the foraminiferal one.

**The key dataset:** *Unraveling the chemical composition of modern, resistant, organic-walled
dinoflagellate cysts via FTIR spectroscopy* — **216 ATR-FTIR spectra, 10 families, 25 genera, 51
species, 17 localities.** Same technique as Loron et al.

Findings that matter here:

- Dinosporin is **"more variable than previously thought,"** requiring **four distinct spectrochemical
  groups**. It is best understood as "a suite of related but chemically distinguishable biopolymers,"
  and species *within the same genus* differ.
- **Some groups show "striking similarities to other resistant biomacromolecules such as sporopollenin
  and algaenan."**
- **Eumelanin** is likely present in coloured cysts, acting as a UV screen.

### Why this helps the hypothesis

It is a proof of concept, and a good one. **Protists demonstrably evolve bespoke, highly resistant wall
biopolymers, taxon-specific down to congeners, and some of them converge on plant-type resistant
chemistry.** Dinosporin is "a completely different biomacromolecule" from algaenan — dinoflagellates
invented their own.

So the objection "no protist builds a sporopollenin-like wall" is simply false, and the broader
objection "a rhizarian could not evolve a novel resistant wall polymer" loses most of its force. Until
dinosporin was characterised, nobody knew dinoflagellates did this either.

The **eumelanin** result reinforces the aromatic-protein direction in
[`aromatic-protein-hypothesis.md`](aromatic-protein-hypothesis.md): melanin is tyrosine-derived, and
protists do incorporate tyrosine-derived aromatic polymer into resistant walls.

### Why it does not close the case

> **"Nitrogen is present in all observed dinosporin types."**

Every one of the four spectrochemical groups — including the sporopollenin-like ones — retains
nitrogen. And the dinosporin backbone is described as "a heavily cross-linked, **N-containing**,
cellulose-like macromolecule."

That is the wrong direction on **both** of *P. taiti*'s defining absences: it is N-bearing, and it is
carbohydrate-based, while *P. taiti* shows neither the amide signature nor the polysaccharide band
ensemble at 1200–800 cm⁻¹.

Melanin does not rescue this either — melanin is itself nitrogen-containing (indole nitrogen).

---

## 3. The closest chemical match is a land-plant polymer

Loron et al. describe the *P. taiti* macerate as containing "short/branched aliphatic moieties,
carbonyl/carboxyl, aromatic, and ether compounds."

**Sporopollenin** is composed of "phenolic, alkane, alkene, ketone, lactone, and carboxylic acid
groups" — the closest published match to that description among known resistant biopolymers.

But sporopollenin's **polyketide biosynthetic pathway appears specific to land plants
(Embryophyta)**. And **algaenan**, the sporopollenin-like biopolymer of some chlorophytes, is
aliphatic-dominated — whereas *P. taiti* sits at the aliphatic-poor end (CH₂ 0.2493, against 0.7095 for
cyanobacteria and 0.4047 for plants).

So the nearest analogue by composition is embryophyte chemistry, which is consistent with *P. taiti*
plotting near plants in the CCA and offers no protist route.

---

## 4. A parallel worth noting

**Chitinozoans** — Palaeozoic, flask-shaped, organic-walled, produced by "an as yet unknown organism,"
with a wall historically called **"pseudochitin"**: a chemically undefined placeholder, exactly like
tectin. An organic-walled Palaeozoic problematicum whose chemistry was never resolved and whose
affinity remains open. It is a reminder that this class of problem has a poor track record of being
solved by wall chemistry alone.

---

## 5. The most valuable thing this turned up

**The 216-spectrum dinocyst ATR-FTIR dataset is a directly comparable protist-wall comparison group.**

The taphonomically matched protist control in Loron et al. is **n = 3** (*Palaeoleptochlamys*), and I
showed in [`data-availability.md`](data-availability.md) §1.1 that only Amide I separates cleanly at
that sample size. Reducing the dinocyst spectra to the same six band intensities would give a protist
resistant-wall comparison set two orders of magnitude larger.

Caveat, and it is the same one as always: **these are modern cysts, not 407 Ma silicified fossils.**
The comparison is only legitimate after artificial maturation, or with an explicit maturity correction.
But it is the largest existing body of ATR-FTIR data on protist resistant walls, and nobody has put it
next to *Prototaxites*.

---

## 6. Verdict

**No existing analogue matches.** Nothing in Rhizaria comes close; dinosporin, the best protist
candidate, is N-bearing and carbohydrate-based in all four of its chemical variants; and the closest
compositional match to *P. taiti* is sporopollenin, a land-plant polymer.

**But the search materially improves the hypothesis's position on one point.** "A protist could not
evolve a novel, plant-like resistant wall polymer" is refuted — dinoflagellates did exactly that, with
four distinct chemistries, some sporopollenin-like, and one incorporating a tyrosine-derived aromatic
pigment.

What survives as the constraint is narrower and sharper than before: **not "protists don't make
resistant aromatic walls," but "every protist resistant wall so far characterised retains nitrogen."**
That is the axis the whole disagreement collapsed onto in
[`tectin-variants.md`](tectin-variants.md), and it is where the argument now lives.
