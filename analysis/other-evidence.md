# Other data that could support or refute the hypothesis

Everything so far has argued over one line of evidence: FTIR wall chemistry. That is a narrow base for
a phylogenetic claim, and it is the line where the hypothesis is weakest — which makes it the wrong
place to keep fighting. Below are eleven independent lines, ordered by how much can be done with data
that already exists.

---

## Tier 1 — public data, analysis not yet done

### 1. Network topology from the published 3D reconstructions ← **the best test available**

The 2026 dataset (**doi:10.7488/ds/7895**) contains confocal laser scanning microscopy **3D
reconstructions of the tube branching networks**, plus photogrammetry models. Nobody has analysed them
quantitatively.

This matters because the observation that motivated this whole hypothesis — Loron et al.'s statement
that medullary-spot branching is "three dimensional and highly complex on the micron scale" with **no
discernible branching hierarchy** — is entirely *qualitative*. It has never been measured.

Network analysis would make it a number. Extractable metrics:

- **Branch angle distribution** — fungal hyphae branch at characteristic acute angles; reticulopodial
  and granellare networks do not
- **Anastomosis frequency and fusion morphology** — rare and morphologically specific in fungi,
  pervasive in granuloreticulosans
- **Hierarchy** — Strahler ordering, parent/daughter diameter ratios. Apical growth produces
  hierarchy; a reticulate network does not
- **Loop/cycle density, node degree distribution, fractal dimension**

**Comparators already published:**

| Comparator | Source |
|---|---|
| Xenophyophore granellare, 3D | Gooday et al. 2018 micro-CT, *Sci. Rep.* (PMC6092355) |
| Fungal mycelial networks | Extensive published network-analysis datasets (Fricker, Boddy, Heaton lineage of work on *Phanerochaete* and others) |
| *Physarum* plasmodial networks | Well-characterised, a non-fungal non-rhizarian control |

This is **completely independent of chemistry**, it tests the single strongest pro-hypothesis
observation, and both sides of the comparison are already in public repositories. If *Prototaxites*
branching is statistically hierarchical, the reticulopodial reading dies. If it is not, the hypothesis
gains its first genuinely positive quantitative support.

### 2. The sulfate band — possibly testable on spectra already acquired

Foraminiferal organic linings are **sulphated glycoproteins** (Hedley 1963; Langer 1992). Allen et al.
(2000) note the diagnostic band:

> "The S=O vibration of the sulphate group present in sulphated glycoprotein... may be detected at
> **1250–1230 cm⁻¹** (Sekkal et al., 1993). As foraminiferal linings are suggested to be sulphated
> glycoproteins, detection in all spectra of absorptions in this region should be possible."

Two facts make this immediately actionable:

1. Loron et al. acquired ATR-FTIR over **4000–650 cm⁻¹** — the band is **inside their range**.
2. The words "sulfur," "sulphate" and "sulfate" appear **nowhere in the 2026 paper**.

So the measurement may already exist and simply has never been looked at. A polyphenolic structural
wall has no reason to be sulphated; a foraminiferal one does.

**Caveat, and it is serious:** Rhynie is a hot-spring system with sulfide diagenesis. Organically bound
S must be distinguished from diagenetic sulfide — pair the band analysis with elemental S mapping and
sulfur speciation (XANES would be ideal) rather than relying on either alone.

### 3. Abbott's lignin monomers vs foraminiferal pyrolysis products

Abbott et al. isolated lignin monomers from *Prototaxites* by alkaline oxidation, finding a
distribution **dominated by H-type** (p-hydroxyphenyl) monomers with G (guaiacyl) and S (syringyl)
also detected. Loron et al. explicitly endorse this result.

Meanwhile Ní Fhlaithearta et al. (2013) found **traces of guaiacols and syringols** — G and S — in
foraminiferal organic linings.

**Nobody has compared these two datasets, and they are directly comparable.** H-dominance is unusual:
land-plant lignin is G-dominated (gymnosperm) or G+S (angiosperm), and the foraminiferal traces are
G/S. If *Prototaxites*'s H-dominated signature has no foraminiferal analogue, that is an independent
strike using only published numbers. If foraminiferal linings turn out to carry H-type monomers too,
it is the first real chemical point in the hypothesis's favour.

## Tier 2 — cheap new measurements on accessioned material

Material is locatable: Lyon Collection, University of Aberdeen (Lyon 156 UC1/UC2/UCB1, Lyon 48 UC1 —
uncovered sections already prepared for FTIR); NSC.36 sections and sub-blocks at National Museums
Scotland (G.2024.5.1, G.2024.5.2).

### 4. Bulk elemental C/N/H/S — the crispest single discriminator

**No published elemental analysis of *Prototaxites* could be found.** This is a striking gap, because
nitrogen content settles the wall-chemistry argument far more directly than band-fitting does:

- An allogromiid-grade test is **>60% protein** → high N, low C/N
- A polyphenolic structural wall → very low N, high C/N

EA-IRMS on macerated material would give C/N/H/S and δ¹⁵N in one run, independent of every FTIR
band-assignment assumption the current argument rests on. Cheap, fast, and it tests the thing everyone
has been arguing about indirectly.

### 5. Elemental mapping — Ba, S, Sr, Pb, U

By EDS, µXRF or LA-ICP-MS on the existing thin sections. Loron et al. ran **no elemental geochemistry
at all**.

- **Ba** — barite granellae. Inference is asymmetric: negative is strong, positive is weak (Carboniferous
  fossils were once assigned to Xenophyophorea on Ba that proved diagenetic). Requires discrete 1–5 µm
  crystal habit, not bulk enrichment.
- **S** — per §2 above.
- **Pb, U** — xenophyophores selectively concentrate these alongside Ba.

### 6. TEM of wall fabric

Foraminiferal organic walls are described as **1–2 µm proteinaceous fibres composed of cross-striated
filaments ~0.2 µm in diameter** — the basis of the "collagen nature" claim (Hedley & Wakefield 1967;
Rützler & Richardson 1996).

*Prototaxites* Type 2 tubes have **double-layered walls ~2 µm thick** whose ultrastructural fabric has
never been described. Cross-striated fibrils at that scale would be striking positive support. Anything
else — homogeneous, lamellar, granular — argues against. This is a clean morphological test of a
chemical claim.

### 7. Septal pore ultrastructure

Still the highest-information unexamined character in the fossil. Type 1 tubes carry **75% of body
volume** and are septate with pores; no ultrastructural detail is published. Pore architecture is
phylogenetically informative in fungi (simple pore, dolipore, Woronin bodies), and the geometry would
discriminate a compartmentalised coenocyte from a genuinely multicellular tissue — which bears directly
on the grade-of-organisation argument in [`rhizaria-hypothesis.md`](rhizaria-hypothesis.md) §7.

## Tier 3 — reinterpretation, where the hypothesis could actually win

### 8. Honegger's "polysporous asci"

This is the one place the hypothesis could gain positive evidence rather than merely survive.

Honegger et al. (2018) described a superficial hymenium on *P. taiti* with narrowly cylindrical
polysporous asci and paraphyses. Foraminiferal reproduction also produces **many small propagules
inside the parent**, released through an aperture.

The discriminating question is checkable on existing thin sections: are the propagules **spores**, or
**miniature tests with apertures**? A juvenile foraminiferan has a test and an aperture; an ascospore
does not. Nobody has asked.

Worth noting the fragment lacks organic connection to the vegetative material, so its attribution is
contested for every hypothesis including Loron et al.'s.

### 9. Growth architecture

*Prototaxites* shows growth zones/rings (Hueber; Retallack & Landing). Foraminifera grow by **discrete
chamber addition** — punctuated, with septa and foramina at each step. Xenophyophores grow by
continuous tube extension.

The photogrammetry and CLSM data could test whether growth was accretionary-punctuated or continuous,
and whether growth zones correspond to anything chamber-like. A continuous-growth architecture with no
chamber equivalents argues against any polythalamous reading and constrains which monothalamid
analogues remain.

## Tier 4 — compilable from existing literature

### 10. Depositional environment census

Hueber noted that some *Prototaxites*-bearing successions are **near-shore marine** with transported
land-plant remains. Nobody has systematically compiled occurrences by depositional setting.

A census — marine vs. terrestrial, in situ vs. transported, across the Silurian–Devonian range — would
convert the habitat objection from anecdote into a distribution. If *Prototaxites* is systematically
marine outside Rhynie, the strongest environmental objection to a rhizarian affinity weakens
considerably. If it is systematically terrestrial, it hardens.

### 11. Biomechanical and physiological limits

A quantitative model of what a syncytium can do: cytoplasmic streaming velocities and transport
distances, diffusion limits, nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio scaling, and the mechanical requirements of a
self-supporting column with 1–2 µm walls.

All the dimensional inputs already exist in the published models. The output is a direct test of
whether an 8 m single cell is physically possible on land — which is the hypothesis's largest
unexamined assumption.

## A comparative check worth running: nematophytes

*Nematoplexus rhyniensis* was in the 2023 ATR-FTIR dataset and was found "most similar to plant
fossilization products." *Prototaxites* Type 3 tubes carry **weak annular thickenings**, and banded
tubes are the defining feature of nematophytes such as *Nematasketum*.

If those banded tubes are homologous, the affinity indicated is nematophyte-adjacent, not rhizarian —
and that comparison can be made today from the 2023 spectra at doi:10.7488/ds/3806. It is the most
likely alternative to *both* the fungal and the rhizarian readings, and it has been under-tested.

Also worth reading as counterweight: **Vajda et al.** (ref. 20 in Loron et al.) argued for a *fungal*
affinity on spectroscopic grounds. Loron et al. rebut it as resting on aliphatic C–H signatures alone,
which they consider uninformative across differing diagenetic histories. Whoever is right, it shows the
spectroscopic evidence is contested in both directions.

## Data-hygiene note

The published CCA matrix contains **four spectra labelled "franckbaronia" that carry no group
assignment** and are therefore excluded from all six taxonomic classes in the ordination. They were
measured and then left out of the group structure. Anyone reproducing the analysis should establish
what they are and why they were unassigned before treating the 102-sample matrix as complete.

## Priorities

If only three things get done:

1. **Network topology** on the public 3D data (§1) — independent of chemistry, tests the strongest
   pro-hypothesis observation, needs no new specimens.
2. **Bulk C/N** (§4) — settles the wall-chemistry argument directly instead of by band-fitting, and the
   data does not exist yet.
3. **The sulfate band** (§2) — may already be sitting unexamined in acquired spectra.

Notably, none of the three is another FTIR comparison. The chemistry argument has been run to the end
of its usefulness; these open genuinely new lines.
