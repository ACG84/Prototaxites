# Reading Abbott et al. (1998), Holman et al. (2024) and the Loron main text

Three PDFs obtained and read. One correction confirmed, one correction itself corrected, and one new
finding that materially helps the aromatic-protein hypothesis.

---

## 1. Abbott et al. (1998): the pyrolysis was run on **thermally overmature** material and could not discriminate

The key numbers, which change how this paper should be read.

**Thermal maturity: Ro = 0.77–1.03%** for the *Prototaxites* host rocks — oil window into wet gas.

**The full *Prototaxites* pyrolysate** (Hudwick Dingle, Soxhlet-extracted): C1–C2 alkylbenzenes and
naphthalene prominent; C0–C2 alkylphenols, C3 alkylbenzenes, dibenzofuran, C1 alkylnaphthalenes,
biphenyl, phenanthrene and anthracene in significant amounts. The Gaspé specimen gave "a mixture
consisting **exclusively** of aromatic hydrocarbons and oxygenated aromatics."

**¹³C CP/MAS NMR**: broad resonance at **100–150 ppm** (aromatic C), a shoulder at **155 ppm**
(oxygen-substituted aromatic C), and **0–50 ppm** (saturated acyclic aliphatic C). **No resonance near
170 ppm is described** — that is where amide and carboxyl carbonyl appear.

**Nitrogen is not mentioned anywhere in the paper.** Zero occurrences of nitrogen, amide, protein,
amino, indole, pyrrole, nitrile, pyridine or diketopiperazine.

### Why this is not the test I claimed it was

Abbott states the problem himself:

> "the pyrolysates of the relatively well-preserved *Prototaxites* samples contain abundant alkylphenols
> and alkylbenzenes, **they do not contain methoxyphenols or catechols**"... "the absence of catechols
> (and methoxyphenols) in the flash pyrolysates of *Prototaxites* **could be due to the thermal
> maturation of these samples**... and **does not rule out the presence of a lignin residue**."

And:

> "The aromatic compounds in the *Prototaxites* flash pyrolysates are **also observed in pyrolysates of
> contemporaneous Lower Devonian fossils with unequivocal xylem** (rhyniopsids, zosterophyllopsids,
> and trimerophytopsids)."

At Ro 0.77–1.03% the diagnostic markers are gone. The lignin markers are destroyed, and *Prototaxites*
pyrolysates are indistinguishable from those of genuine vascular plants. **Amide nitrogen would be
long gone at that maturity too** — deamination and peptide hydrolysis run to completion well below the
oil window.

So the absence of N-heterocycles in this dataset is **uninformative**, not evidence. My previous
message treated it as a hit against the aromatic-protein hypothesis. It isn't one.

### And it cuts the other way as well

Abbott's conclusion is hedged far more than the literature that cites it:

> "**It is possible that** the walls of *Prototaxites* and *Pachytheca* contained an extinct polyphenolic
> structural biomacromolecule that was **a failed experiment** during terrestrialization."

Loron et al. render this as "previous results suggest that *Prototaxites* was composed of an 'extinct
polyphenolic structural biomacromolecule' similar to lignin" and say they "find support for" it. That
is firmer than Abbott's own statement, which is a possibility raised from a dataset its author says
cannot rule out lignin and does not separate *Prototaxites* from vascular plants.

**Net: the polyphenol reading rests on less than it appears to, and the aromatic-protein alternative
is not damaged by the 1998 data.**

The ¹³C NMR is the one direct observation bearing on protein — no described amide resonance — and it
carries the identical maturity caveat.

## 2. Correction 2 confirmed definitively

**Holman et al. (2024) contains zero mentions of *Prototaxites* or nematophytes.** Their lignin monomer
work is on bulk Rhynie chert, and they frame H/G/S explicitly as tracheophyte lignin:

> "Lignin is formed from the polymerization of the hydroxycinnamyl alcohols p-coumaryl alcohol,
> coniferyl alcohol, and sinapyl alcohol, to produce hydroxyphenyl (H), guaiacyl (G), and syringyl (S)
> lignins... **H- and G-type lignins are found across all tracheophytes**, while biosynthesis of S-type
> lignin is largely restricted to angiosperms."

The chert is full of land plants. The H-dominance is a statement about **their** lignin.

**The tyrosine argument's empirical hook is gone.** [`aromatic-protein-hypothesis.md`](aromatic-protein-hypothesis.md)
§2 built on H-dominance being anomalous *for Prototaxites*; it was never measured on *Prototaxites*.
The chemistry (tyrosine = p-hydroxyphenylalanine → H-type oxidation products) remains true in
principle and untested in fact.

## 3. New finding: Loron et al. concede their method **cannot see** a tyrosine-derived aromatic polymer

This is the most useful thing in the three PDFs, and it works in the hypothesis's favour. Verbatim:

> "melanin is present across the whole tree of life, reducing its discriminatory power, and is
> **molecularly similar to kerogen**, meaning that **even if present in the cell wall, its signal would
> be hidden by the stronger general kerogen signal**. We conclude that investigation of melanin in
> *P. taiti* offers limited evidence for our analysis."

Melanin is a **tyrosine-derived aromatic biopolymer** — the exact compound class the aromatic-protein
hypothesis proposes. The authors state that such material would be **invisible to their analysis**,
because its signal is subsumed by kerogen.

That is a declared blind spot covering the hypothesis. It does not make the hypothesis true, but it
means the FTIR result cannot exclude it: an aromatic, tyrosine-derived component could be present in
the *P. taiti* wall and produce no distinguishing signal.

Combined with their other concessions — that proteins may have been "lost shortly after the organism's
death," that *P. taiti* may have been "uniquely more liable to change," and that this "limits our
ability to pinpoint the exact molecular composition" — the paper is considerably more cautious about
what its chemistry establishes than its reception has been.

## 4. Also noted: their critique of Vajda et al.

Loron et al. raise three objections to the pro-fungal spectroscopic study: that without sample
preparation detail "it cannot be excluded that the organic signal reported resulted from **resin fixing
the thin section**"; that the reported lack of any need for **baseline correction** is "puzzling"; and
that the analysis rested only on the methyl/methylene ratio R3/2. Worth knowing when weighing the
contrary literature — the spectroscopic case is contested on methodology in both directions.

## 5. Net effect on the hypothesis

| Claim | Before | After reading |
|---|---|---|
| Pyrolysis never run | Wrong | Run in 1998, but at Ro 0.77–1.03% where it cannot discriminate |
| Absence of N-heterocycles counts against protein | Stated last message | **Withdrawn** — no N expected at that maturity regardless |
| H-type dominance supports tyrosine | Central to §2 | **Withdrawn** — never measured on *Prototaxites* |
| FTIR excludes an aromatic protein wall | Assumed | **Weakened** — authors state melanin-type material would be invisible |
| "Extinct polyphenolic biomacromolecule" is established | Treated as established | **Weakened** — Abbott hedges it and says lignin is not ruled out |

The hypothesis lost its best positive evidence and simultaneously lost the evidence against it. It is
now **less supported and less refuted** — which is an honest description of a question that has not
actually been tested on well-preserved material.

**The test that would settle it remains unrun: pyrolysis, TMAH or XPS on the *Rhynie chert* material**,
which is thermally immature, rather than on the coalified Ro ~1% material Abbott had. Loron et al. had
that material and ran only FTIR.
