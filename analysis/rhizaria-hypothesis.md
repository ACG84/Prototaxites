# Is a Rhizarian (Xenophyophorea / Foraminifera) affinity consistent with the data on *Prototaxites*?

An assessment of the hypothesis against Loron et al. (2026), *Science Advances* 12(4):eaec6277,
"*Prototaxites* fossils are structurally and chemically distinct from extinct and extant Fungi."

---

## 1. Verdict up front

| Hypothesis | Status |
|---|---|
| **H1** — *Prototaxites* is a crown xenophyophore | **Falsified.** Fails on at least five independent characters, three of them diagnostic-by-definition for the group. |
| **H2** — *Prototaxites* is a stem-xenophyophore | **Vacuous.** Surviving the H1 falsifiers requires stripping every character that defines Xenophyophorea. What remains is H3. |
| **H3** — *Prototaxites* is a giant terrestrial foraminiferan (monothalamid grade) | **Not falsified, but not supported.** Survives the environmental and body-size objections that kill H1. Fails on wall chemistry, septation, and biomechanics. Currently weaker than the fungal hypotheses it would replace. |
| **H4** — *Prototaxites* was a **giant syncytium** rather than a complexly multicellular organism | **Live, and underexplored.** This is the part of the idea worth keeping. It is a real gap in Loron et al.'s positive argument, independent of whether the organism was a rhizarian. |

The short version: the specific rhizarian identification does not survive contact with the data,
but the *reasoning move* behind it — questioning whether *Prototaxites* was multicellular at all —
lands squarely on the weakest premise in the paper.

---

## 2. Where the paper leaves an opening

Loron et al. exclude fungi on two independent grounds and then reach their positive conclusion by
elimination. The elimination step is one sentence long:

> "Complex multicellularity is known only in three main eukaryotic lineages" — Archaeplastida,
> Stramenopiles, Opisthokonts.

Prior work had excluded prokaryotes, archaeplastids, animals, and laminarialean brown algae. The new
data excludes Fungi from within Opisthokonta. With the trilemma exhausted, the residue is a new
lineage.

**That inference is only as good as its premise.** Rhizaria does not appear anywhere in the paper —
not to be excluded, not to be discussed. It is absent because the trilemma is a trilemma about
*complex multicellularity*, and rhizarians do not do complex multicellularity. They achieve
macroscopic size by a completely different route: a single multinucleate cell that grows a
structurally differentiated body.

Xenophyophores are the existence proof, and it is a good one. *Syringammina fragilissima* reaches
20–24 cm as one cell. Its body is a system of hundreds of branching, anastomosing tubes. It builds
a rigid-ish structure with internal differentiation and regional specialisation. Every intuition that
says "a decimetre-scale differentiated tubular body implies multicellularity" is wrong, and
xenophyophores are why.

So the question "does the *Prototaxites* data actually exclude a giant unicell?" is legitimate and
was never asked. Loron et al. did not answer it, because they never posed it.

The rest of this document asks it.

---

## 3. The comparison, character by character

### 3.1 What *Prototaxites taiti* actually is (Loron et al. 2026)

Specimen NSC.36, Rhynie chert, 407 Ma. Roughly cylindrical, 5.6 cm maximum width, 6.9 cm through the
block; boomerang-shaped in transverse section. A black carbonised layer separates it from the
substrate. Light brown body with evenly distributed dark brown **medullary spots**, 200–600 µm across.

Three tube types:

| | Diameter | Wall | Septation | Branching | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type 1 | ~10 µm | <1 µm, smooth | **septate, with septal pores** | sinuous, occasionally branching | ~75% |
| Type 2 | 20–40 µm | ~2 µm, smooth, **double-layered** | aseptate | unbranched | ~20% |
| Type 3 | ~40 µm | ~2 µm, **weak annular thickenings** | aseptate | unbranched | ~5% |

All three types run parallel to the long axis, and all three interconnect through fine (<1 µm)
branching inside the medullary spots, where branching is three-dimensional, highly complex, and has
**no discernible hierarchy**.

Chemistry: aliphatic, aromatic, carbonyl/carboxyl and ether bands; **no polysaccharide band ensemble**
(1200–800 cm⁻¹); **no sugar–protein recondensation products**. SVM discriminates *Prototaxites* from
Fungi at 91% and from all chitinous specimens at 93% (MCC 0.85). Perylene, the ascomycete biomarker,
is absent from the fossil and present in the surrounding substrate. Methylation ratio CH₃/CH₂ = 0.75
in the body, 0.71 in medullary spots (~C9 chain length in both).

### 3.2 What a xenophyophore actually is

Three components, all of which must be present:

- **Granellare** — the multinucleate plasmodium inside a branching organic tube system. Tube walls are
  described as *"not clearly visible and presumably very thin,"* of *negligible volume*. Branch
  diameters: 50–100 µm in *Syringammina*, widening to 300 µm at anastomoses; 80–300 µm in *Psammina*.
  Aseptate, freely anastomosing. Cytoplasm carries **granellae** — 1–5 µm barite (BaSO₄) crystals.
- **Stercomare** — strings and masses of faecal pellets (stercomata) in an organic membrane, *outside*
  the plasma. **39.5–82.4% of body volume.**
- **Test** — agglutinated foreign particles (**xenophyae**: mineral grains, sponge spicules, dead
  foraminiferal and radiolarian tests) cemented organically. **14.7–58.8% of body volume.**

Granellare itself is only **1.7–5.1%** of the organism.

Ecology: exclusively marine, ~500 m to 10,600 m. No shallow-water, freshwater, or terrestrial
xenophyophore is known.

## 4. The three things that actually matter

### 4.1 The strongest point in favour: the medullary spot branching

This is the real observation behind the hypothesis, and it deserves to be taken seriously.

Loron et al. use the medullary spots as an anti-fungal argument: branching there is three-dimensional,
"highly complex on the micron scale," involves tubes below 1 µm, and shows **no branching hierarchy**.
Fungal hyphae do not do this. Hyphal growth is apically polarised and produces a hierarchical,
tree-like branch order. A dense anastomosing mesh with no parent–daughter relationship is not a
hyphal habit.

It *is*, precisely, a granuloreticulosan habit. Granuloreticulopodia anastomose freely, fuse, and have
no hierarchy by construction. The best living analogue is not a xenophyophore at all but
***Reticulomyxa filosa***, a giant naked foraminiferan of fresh water and damp soil that forms
centimetre-scale plasmodial networks of exactly this topology — and which lives in the right kind of
habitat.

So the character Loron et al. use to reject Fungi is, in isolation, a positive rhizarian signal. That
is a genuine result and it is the part of the user's intuition that survives.

**But it is weak phylogenetic evidence**, for the reason the paper itself gives elsewhere: an
organism that builds a large body out of filaments will converge on filament-network solutions
regardless of ancestry. Anastomosing meshes have evolved in fungi (rhizomorphs), in myxomycetes, in
algae, and in rhizarians. Topology alone does not carry a clade signal.

### 4.2 The medullary spots are *not* stercomare — and the paper's own data shows it

The most xenophyophore-like *macroscopic* feature of *P. taiti* is the medullary spots: discrete,
dark brown, 200–600 µm masses distributed evenly through a tube network. That is, morphologically, a
fair description of stercomata aggregates.

This can be tested without new work. Loron et al.'s synchrotron FTIR microspectroscopy mapped
medullary spots against body tissue and found them **chemically indistinguishable** across
3000–1400 cm⁻¹, with methylation ratios of 0.71 versus 0.75 — the same ~C9 aliphatic chain length.

Faecal pellet masses are digestive residue. They are not chemically identical to the cell's own
structural wall. Stercomare in xenophyophores is visibly and compositionally distinct from granellare;
that is how it is identified. The spots being isochemical with the body means they are made *of the
same stuff as the body*, which is what you expect from a dense branch node and not what you expect
from a waste dump.

**The stercomare interpretation is dead on arrival, using data already in the paper.** Since
stercomare is 40–82% of a xenophyophore by volume, and *P. taiti*'s volume is fully accounted for by
three tube types (75/20/5), there is nowhere left for it to hide.

### 4.3 The chemistry cuts against Rhizaria too — incidentally, but hard

Loron et al. designed their chemical test against chitin. But the discriminating observation is
broader than that, and this is the single most damaging piece of existing evidence for the hypothesis:

> Fungi, arthropods and oomycetes correlate strongly with "bands characteristic of the fossilization
> products resulting from the alteration of **sugar-protein compounds**." *Prototaxites* does not
> show them. Nor does it show the polysaccharide band ensemble at 1200–800 cm⁻¹.

That is not a chitin-specific test. It is a test for **any glycoprotein or mucopolysaccharide wall** —
and that is exactly what foraminiferal organic linings are. Py-GC-MS work on foraminiferal linings
finds protein plus polysaccharide bound in a complex macromolecule, with **chitin derivatives**
present across taxa. A foraminiferan wall run through Loron et al.'s SVM would very likely be
classified with the chitinous group, not with *Prototaxites*.

There is one intriguing crumb on the other side: the same pyrolysis work reports **traces of guaiacols
and syringols** — lignin markers — in foraminiferal linings, and Loron et al. describe *Prototaxites*
as compositionally closest to lignin fossilisation products. But "traces of guaiacols" in a thin
proteinaceous lining is not the same phenomenon as a structural polyphenolic macromolecule
constituting an entire decimetre-scale body, and those traces are plausibly adsorbed sedimentary
organic matter rather than biosynthate. It is a coincidence of vocabulary more than of chemistry.

**And the preservation logic runs the wrong way.** In a xenophyophore, the organic tube sheath is
explicitly of *negligible volume* — micro-CT studies cannot even resolve it. What a fossil
xenophyophore would leave behind is its **grains and its pellets**, the 55–98% of it that is test and
stercomare. What *Prototaxites* preserves is 100% thick-walled organic tubes and no grains and no
pellets. The fossil consists entirely of the component a xenophyophore barely has, and entirely lacks
the components a xenophyophore mostly is.

## 5. What would settle it: four tests, in order of decisiveness

**1. Barium mapping.** µXRF, SEM-EDS or LA-ICP-MS across granellare-candidate regions versus matrix,
looking for 1–5 µm barite crystals. Note the inference is **asymmetric**: a *negative* result is
strong evidence against any xenophyophore affinity, while a *positive* result is weak. Carboniferous
fossils were once assigned to Xenophyophorea on barium enrichment, and the Ba turned out to be
diagenetic. A positive claim must demonstrate discrete crystal habit and intracellular distribution,
not bulk enrichment. Loron et al. ran no elemental geochemistry at all, so this is genuinely open.

**2. Petrographic search for xenophyae.** Rhynie silica permineralisation preserves mineral grains
faithfully. Any deliberately incorporated, size-sorted foreign particles — grains, spores, spicules,
other microfossils — set into tube walls would be immediately visible in thin section. Their reported
absence is already strong; a targeted survey would make it decisive.

**3. Run the SVM against a rhizarian.** The comparative set was 102 samples from six Rhynie organisms —
plants, fungi, arthropods, oomycetes and others. **No rhizarian comparator exists in the analysis.**
ATR-FTIR of foraminiferal organic linings, monothalamid tests and xenophyophore granellare sheaths,
projected into the same canonical space, is the direct test of H3 and it is a tractable experiment.

**4. Ultrastructure of the septal pores.** Type 1 tubes carry 75% of the body volume and are septate
with pores. Pore architecture is phylogenetically informative in fungi (simple pore, dolipore,
Woronin bodies). Whether *Prototaxites* septa are perforate plates, and what their geometry is, would
discriminate a compartmentalised coenocyte from anything else. Loron et al. provide no ultrastructural
detail here, and it may be the most information-dense unexamined character in the fossil.

---

## 6. The septation problem, which cuts both ways

75% of *P. taiti* by volume is **septate tubes with septal pores**. Xenophyophore granellare is
aseptate. A plasmodium that partitions its dominant tube system with regular cross-walls is not a
plasmodium in the rhizarian sense, and this is a serious objection to H1 and H3 alike.

It is worth being fair about the other edge, though. A *perforate* septum is not a cell boundary. Its
functional consequence is cytoplasmic continuity with mechanical subdivision — which is how septate
fungi remain effectively coenocytic, and which is exactly what a very large syncytium would need in
order not to collapse. "Septate with pores" is better read as **compartmentalised coenocyte** than as
**multicellular tissue**.

That reading does not rescue the rhizarian identification. But it does reinforce §7.

---

## 7. What survives: the syncytial-grade critique

Strip out the rhizarian identification and a real objection remains.

Loron et al.'s positive conclusion — a new eukaryotic lineage — rests entirely on the premise that
*Prototaxites* was **complexly multicellular**, which is what confines the search to three supergroups.
Nothing in the paper establishes that premise. The evidence offered is a differentiated tubular body
at decimetre scale, and xenophyophores demonstrate that this is achievable by a single cell.

If *Prototaxites* was instead a giant syncytium or coenocyte, the trilemma dissolves and the search
space reopens across the eukaryotic tree — including Rhizaria, Amoebozoa, and coenocytic lineages
within the supergroups already "excluded," several of which were excluded on grounds specific to
multicellular body plans.

This does not make *Prototaxites* a foraminiferan. It means the paper's headline claim — *no known
lineage* — is stronger than its argument supports, and that "an extinct lineage of complex
multicellular eukaryotes" should probably read "an extinct lineage of macroscopic eukaryotes whose
grade of organisation is undetermined."

That is a smaller claim than the hypothesis started with, but it is a defensible one, and it is
testable by the same septal-pore ultrastructure work listed above.

## 8. Two caveats on the state of the evidence

**The fossil record cuts against the hypothesis, not for it.** As of 2017 there were **no confirmed
fossil xenophyophores of any age**. Palaeopascichnid claims are contested — a 2011 growth-and-
development study concluded *Palaeopascichnus* was probably not a xenophyophore. A poor fossil record
is often invoked as room for a hypothesis to survive in, but it works the other way here: with no
calibrated framework for what a fossil xenophyophore looks like, there is nothing to positively match
*Prototaxites* against. The hypothesis becomes hard to confirm rather than easy.

**Size, honestly handled.** *P. taiti* at 5.6 cm is squarely within xenophyophore range, and the
"8-metre giant protist" objection does not apply to the specimen actually analysed. But *P. loganii*
reached 8 m, and if *Prototaxites* is monophyletic the hypothesis must scale to a free-standing,
self-supporting, metre-diameter terrestrial single cell. There is no precedent for that, and the
biomechanics — a rigid axial column built from a structural polyphenolic polymer — describe the one
thing rhizarians conspicuously never do.

**Unresolved for every hypothesis:** Honegger et al. (2018) described a superficial hymenium on
*P. taiti* with polysporous asci and paraphyses, and assigned it to Ascomycota. If those structures
are real and belong to the organism, no protist hypothesis survives. The fertile fragment, however,
lacks organic connection to the vegetative material, so the observation is contested. Any competing
hypothesis — including Loron et al.'s own — has to say something about it.

---

## 9. Bottom line

The pore-and-tube resemblance is real but shallow, and it is the kind of convergence you should expect
whenever an organism builds a big body out of filaments. Against it stand three characters that are
diagnostic of Xenophyophorea by definition and absent from *Prototaxites* (xenophyae, stercomare, and
an aseptate granellare), a habitat mismatch of 500+ metres of water, and a wall chemistry that
excludes glycoproteins — which is what foraminiferal linings are made of. The medullary spots, the
most promising macroscopic candidate for stercomare, are ruled out by the paper's own synchrotron
data.

The broadened version — Foraminifera generally — is meaningfully harder to kill, because soil and
freshwater monothalamids exist (*Edaphoallogromia australica*, *Reticulomyxa filosa*,
*Haplomyxa saranae*), because crown Foraminifera are old enough (~770 Ma) for a Devonian branch, and
because the non-hierarchical anastomosing branching is a genuine positive. It still fails on wall
chemistry, on septation, and on the biomechanics of a rigid self-supporting body.

What is worth pursuing is not the identification but the grade-of-organisation argument in §7. The
paper never asked whether *Prototaxites* was a single cell, and its central conclusion depends on the
answer being no.
