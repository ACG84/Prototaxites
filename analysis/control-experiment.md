# Running a rhizarian through the SVM: the control problem

The objection that makes this experiment hard is the right one. Loron et al.'s comparative design is
**taphonomically matched by construction** — Rhynie fungi against Rhynie *Prototaxites*, measured in
the same thin sections, with the explicit justification that all specimens "followed similar
taphonomic and diagenetic pathways." That is why their chitin result means anything. Feeding modern
foraminiferal organic linings into that canonical space would compare a fresh biopolymer against a
407 Ma silica-hosted fossilisation product, and any separation you got would be a diagenesis signal,
not a phylogenetic one.

So the question is: **is there a rhizarian, or a rhizarian proxy, preserved the way *Prototaxites* is
preserved?**

---

## 1. The control already exists — and it has already been run

Loron et al. built the framework for the 2026 paper in **Loron, Rodriguez Dzul, Orr, Gromov, Fraser &
McMahon (2023), *Nature Communications* 14:1387**, "Molecular fingerprints resolve affinities of
Rhynie chert organic fossils." That study ran in-situ ATR-FTIR on **49 Rhynie chert fossils**:

| Group | n |
|---|---|
| Cyanobacteria | 9 |
| Arthropod cuticles | 6 |
| Plant spores | 4 |
| Plant cortices | 11 |
| Peronosporomycetes (oomycetes) | 4 |
| Fungi | 10 |
| Nematophytes (*Nematoplexus rhyniensis*) | 2 |
| **Amoebozoans (*Palaeoleptochlamys hassii*)** | **2** |

That last row is the control. ***Palaeoleptochlamys hassii*** (Strullu-Derrien et al. 2019,
*Curr. Biol.*) is an arcellinid testate amoeba from a pond on the Rhynie alluvial plain, with an
**organic, non-agglutinated test**, preserved in the same chert, in the same thin sections, through
the same silicification. It is a phagotrophic protist whose entire preserved substance is a secreted
organic test.

That is, structurally, exactly the taphonomic control you asked for. Not a rhizarian — but a protist
organic test, at the right age, in the right rock, measured on the right instrument.

**And it was carried forward into the 2026 paper.** The 102 comparative samples are:

> plants (n = 37), Fungi (n = 24), arthropods (n = 12), bacteria (cyanobacteria; n = 10),
> Peronosporomycetes (oomycetes; n = 4), **Amoebae (n = 3)**, and *Prototaxites* (n = 12)

### The result

The verbatim finding from the canonical correspondence analysis:

> "Fungi, arthropods, oomycetes, **and amoeba** are all strongly positively correlated with bands
> characteristic of the fossilization products resulting from the alteration of sugar-protein
> compounds, especially the carbonyl and nitrogen moieties."
>
> "By contrast, bacteria are positively correlated with aliphatic moieties. *P. taiti* and, to a
> lesser extent, plants are negatively correlated with aliphatic moieties."

And from the 2023 study, *P. hassii*'s position "suggests a composition enriched in **amine-containing
fossilization products**," consistent with its organic non-agglutinated test.

**This closes the taphonomic escape route.** The obvious defence of a protist hypothesis was: *maybe
sugar–protein recondensation products just don't survive silicification, so their absence in
Prototaxites tells us nothing about a proteinaceous wall.* That defence is now empirically dead. A
protist's organic test, in the same chert, **does** retain an amine- and sugar–protein-rich
fossilisation signature. *Prototaxites* does not. The difference is biological, not diagenetic.

### How hard this hits the foraminiferan hypothesis

Harder than the amoebozoan/rhizarian distance suggests, and the direction is worth being precise
about. An arcellinid test is essentially proteinaceous. A **foraminiferal organic lining is protein
*plus* polysaccharide**, with chitin derivatives — i.e. it carries *more* of the exact chemistry that
drives the sugar–protein correlation, not less. If an arcellinid test lands in the sugar-protein
cluster, a foraminiferal lining should land there at least as firmly.

The control is a proxy rather than the real thing, and the caveats are real — n = 3, Amoebozoa is not
Rhizaria, and a testate amoeba's secreted test is not homologous to a foram lining. But it is a proxy
that runs *against* the hypothesis, and the compositional argument makes a rhizarian result more
extreme rather than less.

## 2. The direct test: Devonian foraminiferal organic linings

If you want an actual rhizarian rather than a proxy, the material exists and it is close to the right
age.

**Bell & Winchester-Seeto (1999), *J. Micropalaeontology* 18:27–43** — "Linings of agglutinated
Foraminifera from the Devonian: taxonomic and biostratigraphic implications":

- **Age: Lochkovian to Frasnian.** Rhynie is Pragian, so Lochkovian–Emsian material brackets it.
- **Localities:** Australia (Victoria, NSW, Queensland, Tasmania), France, Pakistan, Siberia.
- **Recovery:** palynological residues — acid maceration. This matters: it matches Loron et al.'s
  **transmitted benchtop FTIR arm**, which was run on macerated *Prototaxites*, not the in-situ ATR arm.
- **Size:** typically <200 µm.
- **Grade:** the authors classify their specimens in the **Allogromiidae and simple Astrorhizidae**,
  and note the specimens "do not have any agglutinant covering." These are monothalamid-grade,
  organic-walled foraminifera — **precisely the grade the broadened hypothesis requires.**
- New taxa include *Saccammina mea*, *S. wingarri*, *Psammosphaera garraay*, *Hemisphaerammina
  coolamon*, *Reophanus proavitus*, *Thurammina mirrka*.

### The trap in this material

Bell & Winchester-Seeto state plainly:

> "Most of the specimens are **highly thermally mature; they are black** and many specimens are broken
> or compressed."

**Do not run black linings against Rhynie spectra.** High thermal maturity aromatises organic matter
and destroys exactly the amide and sugar–protein bands the test depends on. Overmature foram linings
would return "no sugar–protein signal" — which would look like support for the hypothesis and would be
a pure artefact. This is the single most likely way to get a spuriously positive result, and it is the
specific failure mode your instinct about matched preservation is guarding against.

Any usable specimen must be **maturity-screened first**, by spore colour index / Thermal Alteration
Index on co-occurring palynomorphs, or by Raman carbon geothermometry, and matched to Rhynie's low
maturity. That means sourcing low-TAI Devonian palynological assemblages specifically, not taking
whatever residues are on the shelf.

---

## 3. The design that avoids the sourcing problem: experimental taphonomy

The cleanest route, and the one that does not depend on finding a low-maturity Devonian foram lining:

1. Take **modern foraminiferal organic linings** (isolated by decalcification) and, if obtainable,
   **xenophyophore granellare sheath** material.
2. Subject them to **artificial maturation** — confined/hydrous pyrolysis, optionally with
   experimental silicification — to a maturity calibrated against the Rhynie fossils themselves
   (Raman, or the published band ratios: CH₃/CH₂ ≈ 0.71–0.75, ~C9 chain length).
3. Run ATR-FTIR under the same acquisition conditions and **project into the published canonical space**.

This is established practice for arthropod cuticle and plant tissue in organic taphonomy, and it turns
an unavailable-fossil problem into a bench experiment. The maturity calibration is the whole game.

---

## 4. What you can do today, for free

The raw spectra are public.

- **2023 comparative spectra** (including *Palaeoleptochlamys hassii*): Edinburgh DataShare,
  **doi:10.7488/ds/3806**.
- **2026 *P. taiti* imaging data** (photogrammetry, CLSM 3D tube reconstructions):
  **doi:10.7488/ds/7895**. Note this deposit is imaging; the 2026 FTIR spectra are separate.

The zeroth-pass experiment: pull the *P. hassii* spectra from 10.7488/ds/3806, reproduce the CCA, and
look at where the amoeba sits relative to *Prototaxites* and to the chitinous cluster in the
projection. No lab, no permits, no specimen loans. If the amoeba plots with the chitinous/amine group
and far from *Prototaxites* — which the published wording says it does — the protist-organic-test
reading is in serious trouble before anyone books instrument time.

## 5. Two routes that look promising and are not

**Radiolaria.** Genuinely Rhizaria — sister group to Foraminifera — and abundant in Devonian cherts,
so both clade and preservation medium are right. But what survives is the opaline silica skeleton;
the organic central capsule does not. There is no organic wall to run. Wrong analyte.

**Silicified Ediacaran "xenophyophores."** *Palaeopascichnus* from South China cherts is silicified and
has been compared to *Aschemonella*. But its xenophyophore affinity is exactly what is contested —
Antcliffe, Gooday & Brasier (2011) concluded from developmental analysis that it probably is not one.
Using a disputed xenophyophore to calibrate what a xenophyophore looks like is circular.

---

## 6. Where this leaves the hypothesis

Before this section, the broadened foraminiferan hypothesis was *not falsified but not supported*. It
is now **materially worse off**, because the taphonomically matched protist control was already in the
dataset and *Prototaxites* separates from it on the sugar–protein axis. The absence of protein and
polysaccharide fossilisation products in *P. taiti* is a real biological signal, not a preservation
artefact, and foraminiferal linings are made of protein and polysaccharide.

The hypothesis is still rescuable — you can posit a terrestrial foraminiferan that abandoned the
glycoprotein lining and evolved a structural polyphenolic wall instead. But that rescue has no living
analogue, no fossil precedent, and no independent motivation, and it strips away the last chemical
character the hypothesis could have been tested on. Each rescue of this kind buys survival by
spending content.

The honest position: the *grade-of-organisation* critique in
[`rhizaria-hypothesis.md`](rhizaria-hypothesis.md) §7 survives all of this untouched, because it does
not depend on *Prototaxites* being a rhizarian. The specific foraminiferan identification is now
carrying a chemical result it has to explain away rather than one it predicts.
